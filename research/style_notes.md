# Style notes

## 2026-10-30
- Contrast hooks resonate: strong data vs. weak market reaction (growth beat, yet stocks fall).
- Single big number plus a consequence (3.4% GDP, 3.6% PCE, Alphabet worst day) beats abstract framing.
- Calm, factual tone with a clear "what changes next" works; avoid hype words.
(Note: trend study used search summaries only; CNBC front page was not fetchable.)

## 2026-10-31
- Event-countdown framing ("Tuesday vote") with a market consequence draws attention; CNBC and Reuters both led with what investors expect before the midterms.
- Attributed numbers (survey share, analyst estimate) read as credible; label them clearly as forecasts.
- Neutral, consequence-first tone: focus on gridlock, deficits, AI rules and the Fed, not on who wins.
(Note: trend study used search summaries and two fetched articles; engagement figures not visible. Metrics show 0 likes/comments on recent posts, so no hook family leads.)

## 2026-11-01
- Election-night market reaction stories lead with a concrete move (index or bank decline) tied to an unresolved event.
- "Who governs next" uncertainty framed as a market consequence, with provisional counts clearly labeled.
- Calm, neutral tone; attribute causes to named analysts, never to a side.

## 2026-11-04

- Election-night stories pair one concrete market number with an unresolved event, never naming a winner as settled.
- Calm, attributed tone: name the firm or analyst behind each estimate (JPMorgan, Pepperstone) and flag what is priced in.

## 2026-11-05
- Contrast hooks keep leading: the feared dip (analyst warnings) vs. the actual big rally; CNBC front page used a cause-and-effect sentence ("X after Y").
- Post-election stories pair one record-style number (best day since...) with strategist caution not to overreact.
- Calm, attributed tone: name the firm (Capital Economics, LPL, UBS, HSBC) behind each view; stay on markets, never on who is better.
(Note: based on CNBC front page fetch, CBS/CNN/CNBC articles; no engagement figures visible.)

## 2026-11-06
- Reversal hooks lead: "rally obliterated" (CNBC Investing Club) and "AI trade meltdown" pair a big prior gain with a bigger next-day loss.
- Cause-and-effect framing: one headline number plus its named driver (AI spending doubts, hedge-fund profit-taking, midterm regulation fears), with a "what's next" jobs-report/Fed angle.
- Calm, attributed tone: name the source for each view (CME FedWatch odds, CNBC, Reuters) and flag that figures differ slightly by outlet.
