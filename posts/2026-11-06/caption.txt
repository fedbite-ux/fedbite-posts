Wall Street week in review: Nasdaq correction, midterm rally erased and Fed rate hike odds in focus

The Dow gained about 3% on Wednesday, Nov 4, after Democrats won control of Congress. One day later, on Thursday, Nov 5, the rally was gone: the Nasdaq fell 5.5% to 24,315 and entered a correction (a drop of 10% or more from a record high). The S&P 500 fell 4.4% and the Dow fell about 3.4%, the worst one-day moves for all three since April 2025. Reports pointed to an AI and chip stock selloff, profit-taking by funds, and fears a Democratic Congress could slow AI data centers.

Oil moved too. Brent fell 8.3% on Wednesday to below $100 for the first time since Oct 7 on hopes for an end to the Iran war, and WTI crude steadied near $90 on Thursday as the outlook stayed uncertain. President Trump blamed Democrats for Thursday's selloff in a Truth Social post; Reuters and CNBC reported that AI spending doubts were already pressuring tech stocks before the election.

The Fed is the other piece. On Oct 28 it held its target range at 3.75% to 4% in a 10-2 vote, and both dissenters wanted a quarter-point hike. As of Nov 5, CME FedWatch showed roughly a two-in-three market-implied chance of a hike on Dec 9. That is a market estimate, not a Fed decision.

What to watch next: the October jobs report today (Nov 6, 8:30 a.m. ET; economists expected roughly 59,000 to 65,000 jobs and a 4.2% unemployment rate), Fed minutes on Nov 18, and the Fed decision on Dec 9.
