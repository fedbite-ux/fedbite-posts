Producer price inflation (PPI): monthly gain cooled to 0.3%, but the yearly rate hit 6.5%, the highest since November 2022, ahead of the Fed's December rate decision.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said producer prices, which measure what businesses receive for goods and services, rose 0.3% in October, a bit below the 0.4% economists expected (Dow Jones consensus via CNBC). Over 12 months the index rose 6.5%. Goods were up 10.2% from a year earlier and services 5.0%. September was revised up to +1.4%.

Why it matters: producer prices can feed into what consumers pay later. After the October CPI report on Nov 10 (+0.4% monthly), Fed officials have flagged that fuel and tariff costs may be spreading. As of Nov 13, CME FedWatch showed about 87% market-implied odds of a quarter-point hike on Dec 8 to 9 (a market estimate, not a Fed decision).

What to watch: Fed minutes from the October meeting on Nov 18 (2 p.m. ET), the Fed meeting on Dec 8 to 9, and November CPI on Dec 10.

Which matters more for the Fed in December: cooler monthly data or the high yearly rate?
