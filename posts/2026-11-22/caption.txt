Credit card interest rates: Trump says he will work with Democrats on a 10% cap (Nov 21, 2026).

On Saturday, Nov 21, President Trump said on Fox News that he would work with the incoming Democratic Congress to cap credit card interest rates at 10%. He first called for a one-year cap in January, but no federal cap has been enacted. The average rate on card accounts that pay interest was 22.36% in Q3 2026 (preliminary Federal Reserve data), and card balances were about $1.285 trillion, according to the New York Fed.

Why it matters: banks oppose a cap and warn it could mean lower credit limits or closed accounts for riskier borrowers. Researchers cited by AP estimate a 10% cap could save consumers about $100 billion a year in interest, though perks and rewards might shrink. A cap would still need to pass Congress, so today it is a political proposal, not a rule. Democrats take control of both chambers in January.

What to watch next: the Fed meets Dec 8-9, the November CPI inflation report is due Dec 10, and the new Congress is seated in January. Market expectations for a Fed hike are a forecast, not a decision.
