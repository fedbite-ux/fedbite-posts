Existing home sales fell to a 16-year low in October, and mortgage rates are still above 7%.

The National Association of Realtors (NAR) reported on Nov 12 that existing-home sales fell 5.5% in October to a 3.75 million annual pace, the lowest since August 2010 and below the roughly 3.85 million economists expected. Sales were down 8.8% from a year earlier.

The 30-year mortgage rate is easing but still high. Freddie Mac's weekly average was 7.11% on Nov 12, its fourth straight weekly decline, versus 7.18% a week earlier and 6.24% a year ago. Prices are still edging up: the median was $416,700, up 0.4% from a year ago, and supply reached 5.1 months, the highest since November 2015.

Why it matters: high borrowing costs keep buyers out and many owners from selling, so analysts quoted by Real Estate News expect sales to stay near long-time lows for the next few months. That is an outlook, not a certainty. What to watch: Freddie Mac's weekly rate each Thursday, the Fed meeting on Dec 8-9, and NAR's November report in December.

Which matters more for the housing market right now: lower mortgage rates or more homes for sale?
