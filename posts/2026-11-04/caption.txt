US stock futures slipped about 0.5% after Election Day as early midterm results favored Democrats, even though the S&P 500 and Nasdaq closed higher on Nov 3.

What happened: The S&P 500 rose 0.36% to 7,568.92 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.83%, while the Dow fell 0.38%. In the evening, S&P 500 futures were down about 0.5% and Nasdaq-100 futures about 0.9% (CNBC, 7:34 p.m. ET). The 10-year Treasury yield was about 4.91% at the close. Control of Congress was not final when this was written.

Why it matters: JPMorgan estimated a Democratic sweep could weigh on the S&P 500 by about 0.5% on Wednesday, with AI-related stocks most exposed to regulation worries. Pepperstone's Chris Weston said a narrow Democratic win may already be largely priced in. These are analyst estimates, not outcomes.

What to watch next: final House and Senate calls, US productivity data on Nov 5, and the October jobs report on Nov 6 at 8:30 a.m. ET.
