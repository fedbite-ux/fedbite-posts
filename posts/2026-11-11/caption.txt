October CPI inflation came in hotter than forecast, and a bigger Fed rate hike in December is now on the table.

What happened: the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Nov 10 that consumer prices rose 0.4% in October (seasonally adjusted), above the 0.3% economists expected. Core prices, which exclude food and energy, rose 0.3% vs 0.2% expected. Energy rose 0.7%, food 0.5% and shelter 0.3%. BLS did not publish most yearly rates because October 2025 data was never collected during the shutdown. Stocks fell and Treasury yields rose after the release.

Why it matters: the Fed held its rate at 3.75% to 4% on Oct 28. Before the report, Governor Christopher Waller said core inflation near 0.3% could lead him to favor a half-point hike in December. Market-implied odds (CME FedWatch, Nov 10): a quarter-point hike fully priced in, about a 13.6% chance of a half-point move. Odds differ by outlet and change fast; they are not forecasts.

What to watch: producer prices (PPI) on Nov 13, Fed speakers on hike size, and the Fed meeting on Dec 8 to 9.

Which matters more for the Fed in December: stubborn core inflation or a slowing stock market?
